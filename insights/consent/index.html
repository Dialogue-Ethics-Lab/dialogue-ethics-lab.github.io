Consent

We recommend that shared society programs shift toward a model of ongoing consent that recognizes participants', including young people's, ownership over their likeness. Rather than treating consent as a single event, programs should understand it as a continuous, revisitable process that evolves as participants' circumstances, comfort levels, and needs change.

SummaryPolicy RecommendationsActivitiesFrameworks

A person’s likeness are representations that create an extension of the self, like audio recordings, videos, photos, etc. Since these are all extensions of the self, they are a part of that individual and belong to them. That individual, no matter age and ability, should have the power to be in control of their likeness, and therefore, must be asked for consent when their likeness is used by another.

Consent is a powerful practice that enables individuals and communities to establish safety, autonomy, and dignity. It is about setting boundaries and having those boundaries be listened to and then respected. Consent is relational, contextual, and dependent on specifics. For these reasons, consent requires consistent revisiting to re-assess if consent has been given.

Why We Discussed This Topic

  • Youth repeatedly asking directors and staff for changes around their likeness, but these requests often went unheard or were perceived as a problem, rather than seeing the problematic policies around youth likeness.
  • Youth participants felt their voice was diminished due to the pressure they felt to please staff members in order to be accepted or receive opportunities.
  • Co-researchers also explored the relationship between consent, one’s likeness, and surveillance, where individuals’ behaviors and actions are monitored and potentially used against them by those in positions of power. They described surveillance, or the feeling of being surveilled, as a common experience for Palestinian staff and youth. They also noted that surveillance is an intersectional issue that can affect additional identities beyond the Palestinian and Israeli national identity binary, like staff and youth who belong to minority groups, such as People of Color and LGBTQ+.
